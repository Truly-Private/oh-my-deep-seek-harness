Build a complete, polished browser game with Three.js. Give it an original name and visual identity; it may be inspired by falling-block puzzle games and the spatial challenge of fitting pieces around obstacles, but do not copy another game's branding, assets, sounds, or interface.

Core game

- Render an inviting 3D 6×6 board with exactly seven pegs occupying seven distinct cells. Generate a new solvable peg layout for every round.
- Use a finite set of 3D polyomino-style pieces that can cover every unblocked cell exactly when correctly arranged. Pieces must have readable depth, lighting, shadows, and distinct materials or colors.
- Present one piece at a time above the board. The player cannot choose or reorder the incoming piece. It descends on a visible timer, creating real pressure without becoming unfair.
- Let the player move and rotate the incoming piece. Show a clear translucent ghost on the board whenever the current orientation has a valid landing position.
- A manual drop or the fall timer places the piece at the current valid ghost position. If the timer expires without a valid player-selected position, the game chooses a valid remaining placement when one exists.
- Already placed pieces remain draggable or otherwise selectable, rotatable, and replaceable. Moving a placed piece must preserve collision and board-boundary rules.
- If the timer expires and the piece has no valid placement, animate the board tipping over and the pieces bursting outward, then end the run with an unmistakable loss state and a quick restart path.
- Completing the board awards points based primarily on completion speed, with a visible round bonus and running total, then immediately creates a fresh solvable board and continues endlessly with a new peg layout and piece order.

Friend challenges

- Make randomness seedable. The URL query `?seed=<integer>` must reproduce the peg layouts and piece stream for a challenge.
- Provide a fully local challenge flow that works without accounts or a backend: create/copy a challenge link, show the current seed, store personal bests locally, and produce a concise shareable score summary. You may add an optional online leaderboard design, but the complete game and challenge loop must work offline after installation.
- Make the score comparable: include completed boards, total score, elapsed time, and seed in the result summary.

Interaction and presentation

- Support mouse and touch. Add clear keyboard controls and show them in the interface. Prevent accidental page scrolling during board interaction.
- Give the game a strong creative art direction, satisfying movement, responsive feedback, and restrained sound with a mute control. Favor clarity over excessive effects.
- Keep the camera and controls usable on desktop and mobile. Provide reduced-motion behavior and do not rely on color alone for valid/invalid placement feedback.
- Include a short first-run explanation, pause/resume, restart, and visible incoming-piece timer.
